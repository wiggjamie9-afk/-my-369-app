Present, not perfect.

A note from Jamie ·

Here's the thing I wish someone had told me earlier: the best talks happen sideways. Not across a table with a "so, how was your day?" that gets a shrug. They happen in the car at traffic lights, at bedtime with the light off, on a muddy walk when nobody's really looking at each other.

Kids open up when the pressure's off. A good question, dropped in at the right moment, does more than any lecture ever will. You don't need to be clever or have the answers — you just need to be curious about the small person in front of you.

These 100 cards are the questions I keep coming back to. Some are silly, some go deep, and a few will surprise you with where they lead. Pull one, ask it, and then — this is the important bit — really listen. You'll learn things about your kid you'd never have thought to ask.

You don't have to get it perfect. You just have to show up and be there.

— Jamie

How to use the deck ·

There's no right way. Keep the cards somewhere handy and reach for one when the moment's right:

  1. At dinner. Pull one card and everyone answers — grown-ups too. Fair's fair.
  2. In the car. Hand the deck to whoever's in the back and let them pick. Side-by-side beats face-to-face.
  3. At bedtime. One quiet question as the lights go down. End the day on a real conversation.
  4. Follow the thread. If an answer opens a door, walk through it. Ask "why?" and "what else?" There's no rush to the next card.

Tip: print double-sided card stock, cut along the dashed lines, and pop them in a jar or tin.
