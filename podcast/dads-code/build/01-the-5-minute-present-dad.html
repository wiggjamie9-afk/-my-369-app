The why

You don't need to be a perfect dad. You don't need more hours, more patience, or more energy than you've got. You need five minutes — and the willingness to actually be in them.

I'm Jamie, a dad of four who spent years feeling like I was failing. Here's what actually moved the needle.

Why this works — the researchIt isn't about doing more

  • Kids don't need more time — they need attention. Psychologists call the small everyday moments "bids for connection" (Gottman); responding to them is what builds secure attachment.
  • Brief, fully-present moments register more than long, distracted ones.
  • For a burnt-out or neurodivergent dad, tiny and repeatable beats big and occasional — every time.

Five small things

Start tonight

1The 5-Minute Rule

The 5-Minute Rule

Give one child five minutes of your whole attention — phone away, eyes up, their choice of what you do. Five present minutes beats an hour of half-watching.

Why it works Undivided attention signals "you matter," which research links to stronger secure attachment than time alone.

Tonight Say "You've got me for five minutes — what do you want to do?"
2Catch the Bid

Catch the Bid

Kids constantly throw out little "bids" — "Dad, look at this!" Most get half a grunt. Turn toward one bid fully today. Stop, look, react like it matters. To them, it does.

Why it works Gottman found relationships thrive on how often we "turn toward" these small bids, not on grand gestures.

Next "Dad, look" Drop everything for 20 seconds.
3The 6-Word Reset

The 6-Word Reset

When you feel the snap coming, don't push through it. Pause and tell yourself: "They're not giving me a hard time — they're having one." Then respond to the feeling under the behaviour.

Why it works Naming the emotion calms your own stress response, so you react from the thinking brain, not the reflex.

Every time One deep breath before you react.
4Repair Beats Perfect

Repair Beats Perfect

You'll lose it sometimes. Every dad does. What kids remember isn't the slip — it's whether you came back. Apologise to your kid: "I got that wrong, I'm sorry." It teaches more than never failing ever could.

Why it works "Rupture and repair" research shows it's the repair, not flawless parenting, that builds trust and resilience.

Next time you snap Repair it, out loud.
5One Small Ritual

One Small Ritual

Presence sticks when it's a habit. Build one tiny ritual that's yours — a handshake, a song, the "air cuddle" my son and I do (ears pressed together, no words). It costs nothing and they'll carry it forever.

Why it works Predictable rituals create felt safety and become anchors of connection kids return to for years.

This week Invent one two-second ritual.
