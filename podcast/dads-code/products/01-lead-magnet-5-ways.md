## The research (why this works)
- Kids don't need more *time* — they need *attention*. Psychologists call the small everyday moments "bids for connection" (Gottman's research); responding to them is what builds secure attachment.
- Presence beats quantity: brief, fully-present moments register more than long, distracted ones.
- For a burnt-out or neurodivergent dad, *tiny and repeatable* beats *big and occasional* every time.

## THE PDF CONTENT

### Intro
You don't need to be a perfect dad. You don't need more hours, more patience, or more energy than you've got. You need five minutes — and the willingness to actually be *in* them. I'm Jamie, a dad of four who spent years feeling like I was failing. Here's what actually moved the needle. — *Present, not perfect.*

### 1. The 5-Minute Rule
Give one child **five minutes of your whole attention** — phone away, eyes up, their choice of what you do. Five present minutes beats an hour of half-watching. *Micro-action: tonight, say "you've got me for five minutes — what do you want to do?"*

### 2. Catch the Bid
Kids constantly throw out little "bids" — "Dad, look at this!" Most get half a grunt. **Turn toward one bid fully today.** Stop, look, react like it matters. To them, it does. *Micro-action: next "Dad, look" — drop everything for 20 seconds.*

### 3. The 6-Word Reset
When you feel the snap coming, don't push through it. Pause and say to yourself: *"They're not giving me a hard time — they're having one."* Then respond to the feeling under the behaviour. *Micro-action: one deep breath before you react, every time.*

### 4. Repair Beats Perfect
You'll lose it sometimes. Every dad does. What kids remember isn't the slip — it's whether you came back. **Apologise to your kid.** "I got that wrong, I'm sorry." It teaches them more than never failing ever could. *Micro-action: repair the next time you snap — out loud.*

### 5. One Small Ritual
Presence sticks when it's a habit. Build one tiny ritual that's *yours* — a handshake, a song, the "air cuddle" my son and I do (ears pressed together, no words). It costs nothing and they'll carry it forever. *Micro-action: invent one two-second ritual this week.*
