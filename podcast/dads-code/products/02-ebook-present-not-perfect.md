## INTRODUCTION — The Dad I Thought I Had to Be

I grew up with a father whose love arrived as money, not presence. At ten, I made a silent vow: if I ever have kids, I'll be there.

It took me decades — and a lot of falling down — to learn that "being there" was never about being perfect. I chased the perfect-dad image for years and it made me *worse*: tense, distant, performing instead of connecting.

This little book is the field guide I wish someone had handed me. It's blunt, it's short, and it works. Six chapters. Six small shifts. Let's go.

---

## CHAPTER 1 — The Myth of the Perfect Dad

**The story.** For years I measured myself against a dad who doesn't exist — calm always, right always, never tired. Chasing him, I was so busy performing that I stopped actually *connecting* with my kids.

> **THE CODE**
> The perfect-dad image is a trap. Chasing it makes you *less* present, because you're managing your image instead of being with your child.

*The research, lightly:* Winnicott's idea of the **"good-enough" parent** — kids don't need perfect parents, they need consistent, warm, good-enough ones. Consistency beats perfection every time.

**One Thing Today:** Write down one "perfect dad" standard you're going to drop this week. Say it out loud: "I'm letting that one go."

---

## CHAPTER 2 — What "Present" Actually Means

**The story.** I used to think presence meant hours logged — same room, same couch. But I'd be *there* and not there, half on my phone. My son would say "Dad, look!" and I'd miss it.

> **THE CODE**
> Presence isn't hours logged. It's attention given. The bond is built in the small moments you choose to turn toward.

*The research, lightly:* Gottman's **"bids for connection"** — relationships are built (or eroded) in tiny everyday moments. "Dad, look!" is a bid. Turning toward it is the whole game.

**One Thing Today:** Turn toward three bids today — fully, phone down — for one week. Watch what changes.

---

## CHAPTER 3 — The 6-Word Reset

**The story.** I couldn't be present when I was flooded — jaw tight, fuse short. The reframe that saved me came in six words, said under my breath before I reacted.

> **THE CODE**
> You can't parent well while you're flooded. Regulate *your* storm first — the parenting follows. The six words: *"They're not giving me a hard time — they're having one."*

*The research, lightly:* Emotional co-regulation — a calm adult nervous system helps settle a child's. You can't pour calm from a flooded cup.

**One Thing Today:** Before you react next time: one slow breath, then the six words. Just once today.

---

## CHAPTER 4 — When Your Brain Works Differently

**The story.** Diagnosed with ADHD at 47, dyslexic my whole life — I spent years sure I was broken. I wasn't. I just needed systems, not shame.

> **THE CODE**
> Neurodivergent dads don't thrive on willpower — they thrive on external systems. Reminders, routines, and self-forgiveness beat white-knuckling it every time.

*The research, lightly:* For ADHD/dyslexic parents, the real blocker isn't capability — it's **shame**. Outsource memory to systems and the shame loses its grip.

**One Thing Today:** Build one "second brain" system for the thing you keep forgetting — a phone alarm, a whiteboard, a sticky note on the door.

---

## CHAPTER 5 — Repair Beats Perfect

**The story.** I snap. I get it wrong. I've raised my voice and watched my kid's face fall. For a long time I thought that made me a bad dad. It doesn't. What I did *next* did.

> **THE CODE**
> You'll get it wrong. What your kids remember is whether you come back. "I got that wrong, mate. I'm sorry." That's the repair — and it builds more trust than never rupturing at all.

*The research, lightly:* **Rupture and repair** — secure attachment comes from *fixing* the breaks, not avoiding them. The repair is the lesson.

**One Thing Today:** Make one real repair this week, out loud. No "but." Just: "I got that wrong. I'm sorry."

---

## CHAPTER 6 — The Air Cuddle

**The story.** My son whispers "I love you, Dad," and we press our ears together — the air cuddle. It costs nothing. It's ours. And it's the thing he'll remember.

> **THE CODE**
> Tiny rituals are how presence becomes permanent. They cost nothing and outlast everything.

*The research, lightly:* Small, predictable rituals build security and belonging — the everyday repetition matters more than the grand gesture.

**One Thing Today:** Create one two-second ritual that's just yours — a handshake, a word, a press of the ears.

That's the whole code. That's enough.
